# Sexual health, consent, and contraception

This is adult medical education, not diagnosis.

## Consent

Consent must be:

- Clear.
- Voluntary.
- Informed.
- Specific.
- Reversible at any time.

Not consent:

- Silence.
- Lack of resistance.
- Past consent.
- Being in a relationship.
- Going to a private room.
- Intoxication, sleep, coercion, fear, or pressure.

Rule: if either person says stop or appears uncomfortable, stop.

## Pregnancy risk

Pregnancy risk exists when semen or pre-ejaculate may contact vagina/vulva. Withdrawal is unreliable and does not prevent STIs.

Default recommendation when pregnancy is not intended:

- Condom every time, from start to finish.
- Ideally condom plus another mutually agreed effective contraceptive method.

## Condom basics

- Check expiration date and packaging.
- Use a new condom every time.
- Put it on before genital contact.
- Leave tip space and remove air from the tip.
- Do not use two condoms at once.
- Use water-based or silicone-based lubricant with latex condoms; avoid oil-based lubricant.
- After ejaculation, withdraw while holding the base.
- If broken/slipped, discuss emergency contraception, STI testing, and HIV PEP if relevant.

## Emergency contraception

- Used after unprotected sex, condom break/slip, or contraceptive error.
- Generally time-sensitive; seek medical/pharmacy guidance as early as possible.
- Not routine contraception.
- Does not prevent STIs.
- Not the same as abortion medication.

## STI/HIV

Many STIs can be asymptomatic.

Test when:

- New partner.
- Multiple partners.
- Unprotected sex.
- Condom break with risk.
- Partner reports STI.
- Symptoms: discharge, burning urination, ulcers, rash, pain, unusual lesions.

HIV PEP:

- If significant HIV exposure risk, consult a medical institution as soon as possible, ideally within 72 hours.

## HPV vaccine

Men may benefit from HPV vaccination because HPV can cause genital warts and several cancers. Follow local approved age ranges, vaccine availability, and medical guidance. Vaccination does not treat existing infection and does not replace condoms or screening.


This is mature, not awkward.

## Boundaries in advice

Do not provide explicit sexual technique coaching. Keep content health-, consent-, and communication-oriented.
